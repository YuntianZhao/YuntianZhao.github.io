# How to Pass the QE?

Most economics PhD programs require students to pass a qualifying exam (QE). Studying for the QE, depending on the school, can be either easy or hard. While some universities have canceled the QE or allow students with decent grades to skip the exam, the rest maintain the old-school approach: sit down, close the books, and take an exam lasting several hours.

In most cases, the QE itself is not helpful for your future research, especially when it covers a field in which you are certain you will not specialize. Consequently, passing it with minimum effort seems to be the most rewarding strategy. Like every exam, the QE can be prepared for.

The best way to study for the QE is always exam-driven. Find the exam papers from the past ten years, and then collect some statistics about the topics, number of questions, and so on. Next, start with the most frequently tested topics and move toward the least frequently tested ones. Try to rationalize why these topics are important and understand how they are often tested.

Work through the questions topic by topic. In this way, you can complete many similar questions in a short amount of time. You should eventually be able to summarize a topic-specific strategy for each major topic.

Finally, leave at least one past exam untouched. Complete it under timed conditions. This helps you understand the number of questions, the time constraints, and how to manage your time. When you encounter a question you are uncertain about, should you skip it immediately or spend more time trying to solve it? A timed practice exam helps you make this decision.

Most professors, even though they write your exam, do not care about the results per se. They care more about the learning process—that is, the hours you spend studying rather than the hours you spend sitting in the exam room. The exam itself is usually not part of the selection process. You should not worry about it too much.
